Each paperclip marks a small change, a moment where something shifted. They accumulate as you read, a visual record of how far you've come.

Memory fragments don't announce themselves with fanfare. They arrive like paperclips slipped onto a stack — small, metal, easy to miss until you count them.

The first change: a single word corrected in a letter never sent.

The second: a habit paused mid-motion.

The third: a name remembered after years.

The fourth: a door left open that was always closed.

The fifth: a question asked instead of an answer given.

The sixth: a light left on in an empty room.

The seventh: a message drafted but not sent.

The eighth: a path chosen without knowing where it leads.

The ninth: a silence that felt like listening.

The tenth: a small thing returned to its place.

When you reach the bottom, all paperclips are visible — a constellation of small changes, each one necessary, none sufficient alone.

The trail doesn't reset. Next visit, they'll still be there, waiting in the margin.
