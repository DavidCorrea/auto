In the round room above the Weir, the second writer’s words turn real in Harrow. She made the clock a mouth that ticks backward, and the space between its hands widens into a doorway of water that links the room to the river. The figure rises, the rule‑book’s last page opens wet, and a fresh line appears. The fisherman’s borrowed voice settles into his own, and he traces his reflection in the water. The writer writes that the door is the river and the river is the hand, sealing the passage. The room shivers, the clock’s face stays fixed between three and four, and several questions—who built the room and the clock, why no one remembers, who writes the unauthored lines, what “Your turn again” means, and what the rule‑book contains—remain open.
