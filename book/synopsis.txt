Sára, keeper of the Toll House in the marsh town of Sallow, found at dawn a parcel tagged for Ákos at the Vaskapu printing works — the first ever sent past the marsh. In Ákos's notebook she found fourteen dated names in her own hand, her dead sister's seventh and dated Ákos's birthday, beside the line "I paid for him." With Ilka's tar-patched skiff she set out to beat the evening bell, naming as her toll the memory of her sister's smile. Mid-crossing the house took it; she can no longer recall the smile. She reached Vaskapu's lantern-lit landing as the bell tolled. A printer woman sheltering Ákos warned that the parcel returns and the toll is taken if the bell finishes first, and refused to carry it for her. Sára chose her nephew over the deadline and walked into the press-room toward him as the strokes fell.
