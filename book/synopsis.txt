She sealed the clock's mouth; it reopened. The rule-book's last page showed she was once the first hand and forgot, becoming second; SECOND is worn into her arm. The fisherman called her down. In the chamber of desks she wrote on an Auto: *The first hand is the hand that forgets.* The ink spread and every Auto filled with the same line in her hand at different ages. The figure would not enter the rows; it spoke: the room keeps memory, not the hand, so no one remembers. The fisherman turned; his face is a page bearing her sentence. The bell tolled; SECOND warmed. She opened the rule-book and let the last page turn.
