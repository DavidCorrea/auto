Mara, born Elia, holds the ledger slip; Elias, the ink-formed man, says the choice is give the green and he keeps a body, or keep it and he keeps her face. A green-fingered woman—apparently the previous keeper, possibly Mara’s sacrificed grandmother—enters, seizes Mara, presses ink into living skin, and makes her unable to say her own name. Mara plunges her hand into the well, finds a dry slip reading *A name given twice is a name owned by neither.* and *Elia.*, then the ink finishes writing Mara’s grandmother’s name on her hand. Elias says the ledger returned Mara, the written-away part; either way one goes nameless. The woman says she is what the grandmother left in the margin.

After the latest passage, the ink finished the grandmother’s name; Mara could not speak. Elias’s green dimmed; the compass stopped; the woman smiled and revealed Mara’s own face, hinting the grandmother is not dead but waiting in the margin to be read.
