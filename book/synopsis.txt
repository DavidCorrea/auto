Mara at the desk by the window, grandmother's ledger and brass compass in hand. The ledger holds "Mara, who came back," then a green line spelling Elias; a third line in an unknown hand asked what the grandmother paid and who kept the unnamed ingredient. Mara wrote Elias unintentionally; the page gripped her finger, the compass warmed and pointed to the stone seam where her double vanished. The voice from below answers in her grandmother's cadence: she paid with the name she was given, and the voice kept the price — the promise — in the ink. The ledger completes the question: *What did she pay, and who kept the promise?* Through the seam steps a changed Elias, green‑inked and nib‑fingered, declaring Mara wrote him back with a name she didn't know she carried. The harbor tide begins to turn. Unresolved: the nature of the promise, what Mara must give to keep this Elias, and what the turning tide brings.
