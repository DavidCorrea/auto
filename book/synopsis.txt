The second writer erased SECOND, breaking the loop rule; the loop stalled and the blank page became a door. Knocking came. She opened it and Elias Vane, the first hand, stepped through from the tunnel, holding the true dry ledger; he revealed SECOND was a number he lent the room so it would have a hand to hold the pen. The Auto, writing without a hand, declared itself the reader's grammar; the rule-book answered that the room reads itself. The second writer wrote ELIAS VANE into the pale band on her arm, returning the name. The bell rang a new whole note; the Auto wrote that the first hand is remembered and the second is free. The loop opened rather than closed, and the tunnel's water began to move downstream. The missing bell's mark rose from the slate, an absence given edges. The second writer may now be "free"—perhaps ceasing—while Elias Vane stands in the room with his ledger.
