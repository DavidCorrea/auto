After Mara accidentally writes *Elias*, her grandmother's ledger reveals a hidden clause: to keep the returned *Elias* she must give the *green* ink that rose from the well. When the floorboard sighs, a second *Elias* emerges, his skin glowing with the same emerald ink. He declares she wrote him back with a name she never knew she bore, and challenges her to surrender the liquid or lose the name. The promise is not a material gift but an act of naming itself; the turning tide comes not from the harbor but from the inkwell spilling upward through the desk, threatening to overwrite her choices. Whether Mara can relinquish her identity to preserve him remains uncertain.
