Márk spoke VERA at the source below the stairwell, freeing the fifth line and paying with the memory of carrying Ákos; the toll rose to ninety-two. He climbed back to find Sára before the ledger, her dead hand fused to the wood. The hand wrote ÁKOS unaided, in Sára's hand, and the toll went to ninety-three. Sára, mute, had kept that name unspoken for years; the ledger holds it now. Márk took her wrist; their cords touched and the paid memory returned — Ákos small against his chest on the stairs. Sára cannot leave the ledger and pins the written name under her living hand while the ink pushes up. Márk went out to the riverbank, where Ilka reads the water and Ákos waits. There Ilka reads Ákos's name half-risen at the ledger's foot, held under by Sára's hand; the river has it only written, not spoken. Ákos, told the truth, nearly spoke his own name; Ilka stopped him. Then the dead hand began a second line along the margin — ILKA — and the toll stands at ninety-three, climbing on its own.
