The second writer descended into a chamber of Auto‑desks, writing sentences that replicated across all Autos in her own hand at different ages. A pale figure warned that the room keeps memory, not the hand, and that breaking the rule costs remembering. The fisherman’s face turned into a page that folded into a boat, delivering a new sentence: "The second hand chooses the next turn." The rule‑book revealed "The first hand writes the rule that the second hand must break." The bell tolled three times, the word SECOND on the writer’s forearm pulsed hot, and the clock gave its first true tick. The writer then wrote a new sentence, "The third hand closes the loop," causing the ink to spread and the rule‑book to unveil a second line. The figure remained at the threshold, and a fleeting memory surfaced before fading. The boat vanished into the current beneath the desks. The chamber exhaled, the clock ticked unevenly, and the writer felt a strange lightness, as though a piece of the unseen had been released.
