Mara keeps her grandmother's ink recipe (oak gall and something unnamed). She went beneath the harbor and met her double, who offered a trade: finish Mara's name in green ink and keep a door, or surrender the name. Mara refused, wrote "Elias" and "run" in the margin (both vanished), and watched the double leave through a seam in the stone. Above, at a desk by a window, Elias sat with a cold compass and a ledger he had never opened. The promised third bell proved to be the door opening above, the desk shifting, Elias's chair scraping. Below, the tide stood upright, waiting; the last stroke of Mara's name waited on the page like a comma. The compass hummed, Elias spoke Mara's name from above, the comma closed into a full stop, the tide lay down, and the stone door shut. Mara is now at the desk by the window. The ledger lies open in her grandmother's hand — "Mara, who came back" — with a second, still-wet entry forming beneath it. Elias's chair is empty. The compass is cold and points at nothing. Unresolved: who Elias was, what the second entry demands, whether the double is gone, and what her grandmother would not name.
