Sára rowed Ilka’s skiff to Vaskapu and paid her sister’s smile as toll mid‑water just as the bell rang. She gave the oilcloth parcel to Ákos on the eleventh stroke; the bell stopped and nothing returned. Inside the parcel she found a letter in her own hand, undated and unsigned, warning not to deliver because what you carry you pay for. Ákos recognized the notebook hand and recorded the loss of her sister’s smile. The printer woman appeared, confirmed the toll was already paid, and said the house now takes the thing left open. Sára refused to bargain for the skiff, untied it, and rowed back toward Sallow, carrying the letter and notebook. The house’s next toll and the printer woman’s lost name remain unknown.
