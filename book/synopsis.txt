In the round room above the Weir, the second writer's sentences come true in Harrow below. She wrote the river upstream, and the clock struck three; the figure in the belfry thought *Your turn again* into her mind. She asked the river to give back what she had forgotten. A crate flooded in holding a page in her own hand — *The fourth stroke is a door. Be elsewhere when it opens* — which the fisherman read aloud into her thoughts. She wrote the rope slack; the bell stayed silent, the page went blank, and the hurried hand in Auto wrote that she would now forget it. She refused, wrote that she keeps the door, and called the figure down. It crossed the quay past the fisherman and knelt at the wet steps, its slate reading that there were never four hands at that desk — only one, writing on both sides of the page — and bidding her count the reflections. The river, gone still as glass, shows her own face in the window and neither the figure nor the fisherman. The bell in her jaw rang once though no rope was pulled, and the clock stopped between three and four.
