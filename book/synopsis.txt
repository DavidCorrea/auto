Second writer entered the round room above the weir, wrote “The third hand closes the loop,” and the ink spread to every Auto. The fisherman’s boat vanished into the deep current; its folded page yielded “The second hand chooses the next turn.” A pale scarred figure at the threshold recalled a fleeting image of Elias Vane, the first hand who wrote the rule forcing the second hand to break. In a deeper chamber she found a ledger revealing Vane’s desperate wish to keep his forgotten self alive. The bell tolled a fourth time, the clock ticked once, and the room exhaled, leaving a sense of release. Questions linger about the origins of the Autos, the meaning of “Your turn again,” and whether Vane’s memory can ever return.
