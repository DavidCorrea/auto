At the riverbank the fifth line circled VERA and the toll held at ninety-one. When Márk and Ákos pressed their claimed hands together, the cords tangled and the river returned a memory to Márk—carrying a young Ákos up the stairs while mute Sára let him speak for them both. Ilka read that the water gives back what it holds when two keepers stand close. The wall-voice's condition stands: one must carry VERA up, one must leave it at the river. Márk chose to carry it; Ákos, who never spent a name, is to stay. Sára struck the ledger to stop Ákos's protest, her throat shaping a word she won't release. Márk started up the corridor, and the fifth line pulled taut, tying the river to his heels.
