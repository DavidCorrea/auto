Mara descended beneath the harbor and met her double, who offered a trade: complete Mara's name in green ink to keep a door, or let the double take it. Mara refused, writing "Elias" and "run" in the margin, then watched the double ascend through a seam in the stone. The margin words vanished. The final stroke now waits on the page, shaped like a comma, a small dark kiss, the notch on a bell that tells the tide to stop. Elias sits at the desk by the window; a compass in his pocket is cold and points to the window. The third "bell" is not a sound but the door opening above, the desk shifting, and Elias's chair scraping from an unopened ledger. The green ink, the color of harbor low tide, pulses on the page. The tide in the room stands upright, waiting to be let back in. Mara must decide whether the waiting stroke belongs to her or the harbor, and whether the third resonance is heard above, below, or both.
