Mara, descendant of Eleanor Vane, came to the harbor following her grandmother's letters (1892), which claim the tide never drowned Eleanor but chose a successor whose name surfaces when the bell rings twice. A young woman in a sailor's cap — the keeper — gave Mara a tarnished compass pointing to where the first name was spoken. A luminous pearl and the bell's tolls led Mara beneath the pier to a submerged alcove inscribed with hundreds of names. There she found a brass key and, beneath Eleanor Vane's name, her own: *Mara*, dated 1892, "who comes when the bell rings thrice." The key opened a green bronze door with a bell-shaped keyhole. The keeper says the tide appoints rather than takes, that Eleanor ran, and the compass now points down. Water is rising. Elias sits at the desk ashore, unaware. Unresolved: what lies behind the bronze door, why Mara's name predates her, the keeper's identity, and whether Mara descends or flees.
