In the round room above the Weir, the second writer made the stopped clock a mouth that widened into a doorway of water to Harrow. The figure, revealed as builder, opened the wet rule-book, wrote “Count the reflections.” The fisherman spoke with his own voice and traced his reflection; the writer sealed the doorway with “The door is the river, the river is the hand.” The clock resumed forward ticking. The writer wrote “Your turn again” on the slate, making a bridge of light; the fisherman and figure stepped through into a deeper chamber beneath the weir. In the new passage, after sealing, the figure spoke; the writer wrote “I am not the first,” and the fresh ink refused her while the bell rang and the rule-book warmed.
