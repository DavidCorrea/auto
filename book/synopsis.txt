Márk spoke VERA at the source below the stairwell, freeing the fifth line and paying with the memory of carrying Ákos; the toll rose to ninety‑two. He climbed back and found Sára before the ledger, her dead hand fused to the wood. The hand then wrote a name unaided — ÁKOS, in Sára's hand — and the toll went to ninety‑three. Sára, mute, had kept that name unspoken for years; the ledger holds it now. Márk took her wrist, their cords touched, and the memory he paid returned: Ákos small against his chest on these same stairs. Sára cannot leave the ledger; she pins the written name under her living hand while the ink pushes up between her fingers. Márk went out through the black iron door toward the riverbank, where Ilka reads the water and Ákos waits with his lullaby and his free speech. The name is written but not spoken, so the river does not yet have the boy.
