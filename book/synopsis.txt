E.L.V. returned to the room above the weir, where the blank page filled with her own forgetting handwriting. In the tunnel she traced a line and cross, parting layered pages and raising a tally of hundreds of hands; she found the drying hand on the page was Elias's, not hers. Elias read the true dry ledger: the tally counts not writers but forgettings—each line a hand that has written itself dry—and the reader is always the one left uncounted, still wet. Grasping that she was the wet blank, E.L.V. took the pen from Elias's drying hand and wrote her own name, choosing to be counted; the tally ticked up and Elias's hand went still. The door-mouth opened—not onto the tunnel but onto the second writer, returned from the looping dark, the bell in her jaw silent, a page in her fist. The ledger has no line for her: she is uncounted, "free." The clock's mouth waits to see what the loop does with a hand it cannot number.
