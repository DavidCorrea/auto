Sára rowed Ilka's skiff to Vaskapu, paid her sister's smile as toll mid-water, reached the press-room as the bell rang. She gave the oilcloth parcel to Ákos on the eleventh stroke; the bell stopped, nothing came back. The printer woman said it held. Inside was a letter in Sára's own hand, undated, unsigned, fold worn soft; it said she named Ákos's mother and warned not to deliver, for what you carry you pay for. Ákos recognized the notebook hand; Sára found no writer. Ákos wrote the smile's loss into the notebook.

They are now in Vaskapu after the bell, with no ferry and only the skiff. Sára learned the letter was written on the works' laid paper in the press-room and that she forgot the trip. Ákos said she wrote it there. The printer woman appeared and spoke of the toll already paid, the skiff, and the house taking the thing left open. Sára asked what the house does to a toll already paid; the printer woman did not answer fully. Sára holds the letter and notebook; the skiff is tied.
