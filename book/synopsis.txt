In the round room above the Weir, the second writer's sentences become real in Harrow. She wrote the river upstream, the clock struck three, the figure thought *Your turn again*. She asked the river for forgotten things. A crate brought a page in her hand: *The fourth stroke is a door. Be elsewhere when it opens.* The fisherman read it aloud into her mind. She wrote the rope slack, the bell stayed silent, the page went blank, and an unauthored hand wrote she would forget. She refused, wrote she kept the door, called the figure down. The figure knelt at the wet steps; its slate said there was only one hand, writing both sides, and to count reflections. The river went still, showed only her; the bell rang; the clock stopped between three and four.

Now the stopped clock has become a mouth, or a door. The fourth stroke is reachable. She wrote *The fourth stroke is the door and I am not at the door*, then *I am elsewhere*. The ink refused to dry; the hurried hand's line burned. The fisherman finally spoke, with a voice not his own: *You wrote me a door. I came to knock.* The clock between three and four began to tick backward.
