Mara stands at the desk by the window with the ledger open. Her grandmother's oak-gall ink has recorded "Mara, who came back" and a second line has just finished in green, spelling Elias—the name her grandmother ground into the ink, a secret until now. The compass in her palm once cold and pointing at nothing has twitched toward the empty chair where Elias's voice once called Mara from above, and now rests warm but still. The ledger's next entry demands a name given freely; Mara has given Elias's name, but the cost and what comes next remain unclear. The double has vanished through a seam in the stone, the harbor's door is shut, and the tide has fallen. The unresolved question is what the second entry now demands, whether the compass's movement means anything for the ledger's ongoing bargain, and what the grandmother's unnamed ingredient truly was.
