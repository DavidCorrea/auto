Sára carried the faceless doll home and set it on the kitchen table; the iron parcel, light now, sat beside it. Her hands smelled of cut wood; the Wheel under the floorboards had turned in the night. She opened the parcel despite the warning: a gouge worn to her grip, pale shavings, and a child-sized faceless doll tagged for The Point with Ilka Dálnoky as deliverer — in Sára's own cramped hand, the hand of the fourteen names in Ákos's notebook. She makes the dolls by night and forgets by morning; the house returns them at dawn. As the Wheel stopped, the toll took the whole morning: the smokehouse, the bell, the face, the opening. She stands at her table with two faceless dolls and no memory. The sister's face is with the house, unplaced. Ákos keeps the notebook and the Letter at Vaskapu; Ilka is owed the parcel.
