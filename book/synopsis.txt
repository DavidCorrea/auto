Ch 1. In the marsh town of Sallow, Sára Vass, 53, keeps the Toll House at the causeway's end. Every dawn things appear on the kitchen table with paper tags: carry this to that address before the evening bell, or it goes back and the house takes a toll — a memory, a skill, a sense. What you carry, you do not open; the Halászys did and forgot each other's names. An iron wheel under the floorboards clicks one tooth each night; no hand turns it, and no one remembers the work. Sára's nephew Ákos, 17, keeps a notebook of everything taken from her, first line: her own name, gone. This morning there were two things: a doll with its face scraped smooth, tagged for the burned house on the Point, and a packet for Sára herself, saying open. She opened it; inside, a name: VERA. Ákos crossed out the notebook's first line. His letter to the Vaskapu printing works goes unanswered in his pocket. They have the doll, the lantern, the good boots, and low tide at noon, to reach a house empty since the fire the year he was born.
