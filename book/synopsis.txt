At dawn at the Toll House, Sára, Ákos and Ilka carried the blank sixteenth tag from The Point and set it on the table. Ákos wrote the date, a nameless name appeared, and beneath Vera Vass the notebook produced Sára Vass — the keeper's own name — while her voice returned as shaped, silent words and Ákos's touch came back as a faint echo. A new blank tag appeared. A woman arrived with a leather satchel for the second delivery; Ákos took it and opened it himself so Sára would not pay again, and the toll took his memory of his mother's kitchen and her humming. Inside was one of Ilka's dolls, its face sewn to look like Ákos. Sára wrote that he is her son and that he carried her tag across the marsh. The satchel woman, paid to say nothing, reported that her payer had no left hand and had asked whether the keeper was remembering her name. Ilka insists the doll must go to the fire; Ákos refuses until he learns who made it.
