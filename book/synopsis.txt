In the flooded chamber Sára wrote Sebes awake, freed the debt, and lost the room where she was taught; the water dropped, showing steps up, and the cord jumped to Ákos, who holds the ledger. Ilka climbed aboard, cold and heavy, claiming she was the voice that taught Sára and that the river took her for it. Ákos spoke the name: the water fell another step and Kornél lost the name for good, though the ink on Ákos's palm holds it. Sára's living hand began tracing three strokes and a hook; Ilka denies teaching her that, or using any voice, or any room with a window. Ákos tried to write Ilka's name to free her; the dead hand wrote a tally instead and the water rose a step. Ákos left the boat with the ledger and climbed to a landing where a door bears the same mark.
