Elizabeth Vane wrote her name in the ledger's wet blank, choosing to be counted; the tally rose and Elias's hand stilled. The door-mouth opened onto the second writer, returned from looping dark, uncounted and free, holding a page of her own handwriting. She wrote "The tally subtracts now"; the ink spread, Elizabeth's name began fading, Elias's hand warmed, and the clock's mouth yawned wider as the weir sounded like a lock turning. The second writer then wrote, "Elias chose the forgetting before he chose me." Elias felt the pen move inside his hand; the ledger dried from within and its column became blank. She pressed the page; the ink reversed, names bled into the ledger, and the column filled with wet marks before one dry stroke, Elias's hand writing itself dry again. The clock's mouth contracted; the weir turned again. E.L.V. stood between them, the pale warm band pulsing, as the count was taken from her.
