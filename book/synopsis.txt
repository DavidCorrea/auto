The agent carries Ottoline’s sealed letters (three still unbroken) and is bound to the ship *Mercy of Small Hours* by a pulsing blue string and a brass key. In a cabin below decks he found Ottoline’s apprentice — a woman with ink-stained hands and the lighthouse beam’s pale eyes — still writing. She has revealed the letters’ purpose: they are addressed to the harbor’s drowned, and each unread letter is ballast, keeping the *Mercy* and its unquiet dead from sailing. Eleven letters remain unfinished. Elias, the baker and lighthouse keeper’s grandson, has come below and learned that his grandfather wrote secretly to his drowned grandmother for forty years, and that Ottoline finished a letter to her the night of the fire. The crowd on the quay above has gone oddly quiet. Unresolved: what the finished letter says, why the lighthouse burned, whether Elias will read it aloud, and whether the ship will sail with or without the town’s blessing.
