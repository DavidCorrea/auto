Elizabeth Vane wrote her name in the ledger's wet blank to be counted, stilling Elias's hand. The door-mouth opened on the second writer, free and uncounted, who wrote that the tally subtracts and that Elias chose the forgetting before he chose her. Names bled between the ledger and her page; the tally reversed and refilled, ending in one dry stroke of Elias's hand. Then Elizabeth, seeing the count taken from her, took the pen and tried to write the second writer's name instead. The ledger refused it — the ink beaded and rolled off — proving the free hand cannot be numbered. The act cost Elizabeth the last of her own name: she is now in no column at all. The clock's mouth jammed half-closed on a half-made number, and the weir's second turn opened a sluice. Water is rising through the floorboards. Elias holds the ledger, dry and unbothered by the flood, and finds a new line at the top of the column in a hand none of them knows: *The ledger is a mouth too.* The second writer's page floats on the water, its names dissolving.
