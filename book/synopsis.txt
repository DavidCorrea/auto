The writer sealed the doorway of water and wrote "I am not the first," but the ink refused. The bell rang, the rule-book warmed, and its last page rearranged into "The first hand is the one that waits," then "The second hand is the one that learns to wait." The figure took her wrist and told her she had written the refusal before she knew the question. She asked whose the second hand was. Turning her arm, the figure showed the word SECOND worn into her skin, older than her memory, and said she had been the first hand once, and that forgetting had made her second. Meanwhile the clock's mouth opened a finger's width onto river air, unsealed, and the fisherman's own voice rose up the wet steps: "Bring the book down. The first hand is done waiting." The writer took the rule-book from the figure's palm and set her foot on the top step down.
