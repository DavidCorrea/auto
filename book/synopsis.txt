The writer sealed the clock's mouth but it reopened. The rule-book's last page rearranged itself, revealing she was once the first hand but forgot, and forgetting made her second. The word SECOND is worn into her forearm, older than memory. The fisherman's voice called from below: "Bring the book down. The first hand is done waiting." She descended the wet steps with the rule-book, the figure behind her, into a chamber lined with desks—every desk holding an Auto, all blank and waiting. The fisherman sits at the far end. A page on the nearest desk rewrites itself: "I am the first hand. I have waited since before I forgot my name." The bell in her jaw tolled. She now understands the cycle: the waiting, the forgetting, the becoming second without knowing you were first.
