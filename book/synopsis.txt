Mara stands at the edge of the harbor as water rises beneath the pier. She has found her own name—Mara—carved in stone in 1892 beneath Eleanor Vane's, marked with the note that she comes when the bell rings thrice. The brass key opens a green bronze door with a bell-shaped keyhole, revealing a stairwell lined with names. The translucent keeper in the sailor's cap, possibly Eleanor herself, warns her not to descend. Mara steps down anyway, and a voice below speaks her name in her own tone. Elias remains at the desk ashore, unaware. Unresolved: what lies at the foot of the stairs, whether the keeper is Eleanor or an apparition, why Mara's name predates her birth, and whether descending is an appointment or an escape.
