Sára Vass, keeper of the Toll House, rowed to The Point and delivered the iron parcel to Ilka Dálnoky; at the bell her small doll took her sister's face. The crossing cost her the whole morning. Back home she found a dawn tag naming Ákos Vass at Vaskapu as deliverer, and refused — she pocketed tag and doll and told the house it would take from her, not the boy. The Wheel's shavings went cold and ashen. Then a bell rang far off toward Vaskapu, the doll's face was stripped away, and the shavings warmed again: the house had collected from Ákos anyway and charged Sára the sister's face. The tag's ink was still wet — Sára wrote Ákos's name herself and forgot. She has rowed out toward Vaskapu.
