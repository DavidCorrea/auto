Sára Vass keeps the Toll House at Sallow's causeway: at dawn things appear on the kitchen table tagged with an address, to be delivered before the evening bell or the house takes a toll — a memory, a skill, a sense. Nothing carried may be opened. An iron wheel under the floorboards clicks one tooth nightly, turned by no hand. Ákos, her 17-year-old nephew, records every loss in a notebook; her own name was the first.

This morning two things came: a faceless doll for the burned house on the Point, and a packet tagged *open*, containing the name VERA. Sára opened it. They crossed at low tide. On the Point, Ilka Dálnoky, who keeps the yard, called Sára Vera and said she was the fourth to bring a doll; three faceless dolls stand on her shelf, a fourth was swept away. The rule: doll on the small hearthstone in the back room, off the Point before the bell; the doll has a face by evening and Ilka never stays to see whose. Sára delivered it and lost the memory of her sister's face — Ákos's mother. Ákos has decided to leave for Vaskapu on the evening tide.
