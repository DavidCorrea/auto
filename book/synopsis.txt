Sára Vass, keeper of the Toll House, rowed the skiff to The Point and delivered the iron parcel to Ilka Dálnoky. At the bell the small faceless doll she carried took on her sister's face. The crossing cost her the whole morning, which she cannot recall. Back at the Toll House she found a new dawn tag naming Ákos Vass at Vaskapu as the next delivery. Knowing the house takes a face at each bell, and that she once "paid for" Ákos with the sister's face, Sára refused: she pocketed the tag and the doll and told the empty house it would take from her and not the boy. The iron Wheel under the floor went cold and ashen — the house has registered her defiance and is reckoning its cost. Open: why Sára makes and forgets the dolls; the other thirteen names and their costs; who died in the fire and the child of the hearth; the printer woman's lost name; what the house will now take from Sára.
