Sára rowed Ilka's skiff across to Vaskapu, paying her sister's smile as toll mid-water, and reached the press-room as the bell ran. She got the oilcloth parcel into Ákos's hands on the eleventh stroke; the bell stopped and nothing came back for it. The printer woman said she had carried one once and paid her name for it. Inside the parcel was no doll and no tag, only a letter folded three times, in Sára's own hand, undated and unsigned, its fold worn soft. It said she had named Ákos's mother — "you were born blue and silent" — and warned: do not deliver for anyone, what you carry you pay for. Ákos recognised the notebook hand; Sára found no one who wrote it. Ákos wrote the smile's loss into the notebook so it lives somewhere besides her.
