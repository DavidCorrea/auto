Mara, a young woman in an unnamed harbor town, found her name carved in 1892 stone beneath Eleanor Vane's, with the line *who comes when the bell rings thrice*. A brass key opened a green bronze door (bell-shaped keyhole); a translucent keeper in a sailor's cap — possibly Eleanor, drowned — warned her; the compass needle points straight down. The tide does not take, it appoints; Mara's grandmother was appointed and ran. Elias waits ashore at the desk by the window, unaware. Now Mara stands in a dry chamber beneath the harbor: tide upright like glass, a desk, a window onto the pilings, and a woman with Mara's face writing Mara's unfinished name in green ink. The stairs have vanished. Two bells remain. The double offers a trade: Mara finishes the name and keeps the door; the double goes up and becomes her.
