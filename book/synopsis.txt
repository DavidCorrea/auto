Márk, Ákos, Ilka and Sára descend the revealed stair into a flooded stone corridor. Ákos speaks his name, but the ledger does not record it; the walls demand a name for toll eighty‑one. Márk refuses to name a person, calling the debt the barnacled box, and the voice falls silent, the water stops rising. Ilka reads the third line of the parchment aloud; the wax seal breaks, a memory of a lullaby floods her mind and is seized by the ledger as payment, updating the tally to eighty‑two and opening the marked door. Water recedes, revealing a new stone passage. Márk opens the barnacled box set on the landing; its nacre interior is empty, the debt accounted for by the act of opening itself. The chamber sighs, the flood eases, and a dark, passable corridor lies ahead. Sára’s fragment of voice still murmurs “remember.” Open threads remain: Márk’s forgotten life, Ákos’s full restoration, Sára’s toll, and the far end of the stone corridor.
