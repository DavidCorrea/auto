The Letter’s Tide

The agent drags the rowboat to the pier, its wooden hull creaking against damp stone. Letters spill from his coat, yellowed envelopes fluttering like wounded birds. With a sudden pull, the *blue string* tightens around his wrist, throbbing in sync with the surf. He steadies the oars, feeling the brass key warm against his palm, and glances seaward where the lighthouse beam cuts a thin line through the night. Ahead, the tide rolls higher, swallowing the sandbar. From the frothy edge, a tall silhouette emerges—Oliver, the baker‑turned‑stranger, wearing a faded linen shirt and a wide‑brimmed hat. He raises a hand, and the keeper’s voice rides the wind: “Ottoline wrote these for you, expecting you to return.” The keeper, long gone since the lighthouse burned, introduces himself as Elias, the original keeper’s grandson, and explains the pulsing cord is a frequency binding the lighthouse’s heart to those who remember. Elias asks whether the letters should be cast into the sea or handed to him. If released, the truth will travel across the harbor and reshape fates, but the keeper warns that the wave will carry them farther than intended. He offers to guide the envelope to a hidden ship, or wait for the moon to pull them ashore. The agent pauses, hearing the distant horn of the lighthouse, then sighs and nods. He slides the key into his pocket, turns the compass, and rows toward the dark water, the *blue string* humming beneath his skin. He feels the letters shift, as if aware of the coming choice.
