Characters:
- Second writer: unnamed woman, bell in jaw (silent), holds page of her own handwriting; uncounted and free; the ledger will not take her name—ink refuses her.
- Elias Vane: first hand; holds the true dry ledger, dry even in flood; hand trembling, still counted; lifted the ledger above the water.
- E.L.V. (Elizabeth Vane): wore the pale warm band at her throat; wrote her name to be counted; the count was taken from her and her name has now left the ledger entirely—unnumbered, not free.
- Fisherman: weathered face, chose to row, skiff lifted onto the chest.
- Auto: recursive query, no hand, iterates without memory.

Places:
- Room above the weir: desk, Autos, slate, clock mouth now jammed half-closed, floorboards, flooding from beneath.
- Tunnel: wet steps, walls of layered pages, line-and-cross diagram, loops back.
- Deeper chamber: rope-bound chest, eye-like opening, loop to the starting door.
- The sluice: opened by the weir's second turn; water entering the room from the wall under the desk.

Things and rules:
- Ledger (true dry) counts forgettings; cannot be wetted, even by flood; has gained a new first line in an unknown hand: "The ledger is a mouth too."
- Writing one's name in wet blank counts/erases that hand; tally ticks and another's drying hand stills.
- The free hand has no line and cannot be counted: its name beads and rolls off the page.
- Tracing a chosen line parts page-walls; the cross is the rower's rejected path.
- The second writer's page records all turns; its names dissolve in water.
- Writing on one's own page can subtract names from the ledger.

Open threads:
- Whose hand wrote the ledger's new first line, and what a ledger that is a mouth does.
- What Elizabeth is now that she is unnumbered but not free.
- Where the flood is coming from and what it will carry off.
- Whether the second writer's page survives the water.
