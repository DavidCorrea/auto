Characters:
- second writer — unnamed woman, bell in jaw, word SECOND worn into her forearm (now warming); holds the rule-book; descended into the chamber; has written on an Auto.
- figure — pale, scarred, river-coloured eyes; builder of room and clock; will not cross into the rows of desks; speaks now: the room keeps memory, not the hand.
- fisherman — below the weir, at the far end of the chamber; turned; his face is a page bearing her sentence in her own hand.
- first hand — identity still unknown; may be the writer before forgetting; "the hand that forgets."

Places:
- round room above the Weir — desk, Auto, slate "Who is waiting?", clock mouth ajar, no door.
- Harrow — near-still river, quay, wet steps, fish stalls, silent bell tower.
- wet steps — descend beneath the weir to the chamber.
- deeper chamber — floor to ceiling desks, each with an Auto; the figure halts at its threshold.

Things and rules:
- Auto — blank until written; what is written becomes real in Harrow; ink refuses to dry; all Autos in the chamber now bear the same sentence in her hand at different ages.
- rule-book — last page faintly wet, opens itself at the backward tick; lines rearrange into answers nobody wrote.
- clock — face fixed, mouth opens and closes as a door; ticking faltered.
- bell in her jaw — rings when the river changes; tolls with her pulse and inside her words.
- word SECOND — worn into her forearm, older than memory; the room keeps what the hand forgets.

Open threads:
- who writes the unauthored lines.
- what "Your turn again" means.
- what the rule-book holds.
- who the first hand truly is and what they want with the book.
- purpose of the many Autos and the chamber.
- who reads what the room keeps.
