Characters: second writer — unnamed woman, bell in jaw, writes in Auto; what she writes manifests in Harrow. The figure — pale, scarred, river-coloured eyes, carries the original rule-book; in the belfry, rope now slack, rule-book on the sill, slate turned toward her but unread. Fisherman — unnamed man; waded the flood, opened the crate, read the page aloud; his voice reaches her as thought.
Places: round room above the Weir — desk, Auto, slate, clock at three, no door; window onto Harrow. Harrow — river slowed to near-stillness; quay, wet steps, fish stalls, bell tower. Tower belfry — great bell silent, rope hanging slack, figure present.
Things and rules: Auto — blank until written; what is written becomes real outside the window; sentences appear before ink dries; an unknown hurried hand writes lines she did not author. Room's slate — last bore *The writer speaks, the river listens, the clock counts, the tower answers.* Figure's slate/rule-book — many hands, chalk shifts at a tap; last bore *The writer reads. The reader writes.* Bell in her jaw — rings when the river changes, resonates with the tower bell. Pen — warm, writes until ink dries. Tower bell — rings only when the rope is pulled; its stroke advances the clock. Clock — at three, and did not move for her last two sentences. The crate — river-borne, held a dry page in her own hand, which she read and cannot remember writing; its ink lifted away afterward.
Open threads: who built the room and the clock; why no one remembers; what Auto is for and who reads it; whose hands wrote the slate's first rules and what the figure corrects; who writes the lines she does not; who or what "Your turn again" is; what the fourth stroke is and what door it opens; why the clock held at three; what the figure's turned slate says; when she wrote the page.
