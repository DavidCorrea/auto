Characters:
- Sára Vass (Vera), 53, keeper of the Toll House; voice and memory of being Vera taken as toll; awake by choice; shapes dolls from tag ash at dawn unaware; voice returns as shaped words without sound; remembers her true name is Sára; wrote in the notebook that Ákos is her son.
- Ákos Vass, 17, Vaskapu press-room; keeps notebook of losses; cannot feel paper, ash or own writing, only faint echoes; opened the satchel and paid with the memory of his mother's kitchen, her humming and the flour crock; now calls Sára "keeper" and cannot recall her as his mother; determined to find who made the doll.
- Ilka Dálnoky, 70s, Point smokehouse; calls Sára Vera; keeps faceless dolls, one bearing the child's face; says a sewn face can only be undone by fire.
- The satchel woman, unnamed, brought the second delivery; paid to leave it and say nothing; reveals her payer had no left hand.

Places:
- Vaskapu press-room, stone landing with lantern, Ákos's workbench.
- Sallow marsh town, Toll House at causeway's end, table, iron stove, flour crock.
- The Point: roofless burned house, black hearthstone, Ilka's smokehouse.

Things and rules:
1. Tags appear at dawn in Sára's cramped hand, ink often wet; she does not remember writing them.
2. Toll taken from deliverer; opening what you carry costs memory.
3. Dolls take a face at the bell, or from a living face sewn in thread; the small doll warms when a debt is carried, cold on refusal.
4. The Notebook: names in Sára's hand; dates and names appear even when Ákos holds the pen; Ákos feels touch as faint echo when writing; Sára Vass now stands beneath Vera Vass.
5. The Rest: began with the fire at The Point; Ilka's memory was its unpaid fee; the child was inside the fire.
6. Hearthstone: required sixteenth tag; costs voice or last memory; warms when carried; blank tags are lighter than ash.

Open threads:
- The twelfth and earlier names, their costs, and the seventh crossed-out "I paid for him."
- Why Sára chose Ákos and how he lost his touch.
- Sára's doll-making and whether her full identity will return.
- The man with no left hand who sent the satchel and knew the keeper was remembering.
- What breaking or burning the Ákos-doll would do to Ákos himself.
