Characters:
- Sára Vass (Vera), 53, keeper of the Toll House; obeys the house over people, forgets her own acts; wrote Ákos's tag and the fourteen names in her own hand and forgot; now at The Point with Ákos, about to confront Ilka.
- Ákos Vass, 17, Vaskapu press-room; keeps the notebook of losses; lost tactile memory of type at the bell; knows Sára is the ledger who pays the fees; left the notebook behind, came to The Point.
- Ilka Dálnoky, 70s, Point smokehouse; knows Sára as Vera; lent the skiff; took the iron parcel; shelf of faceless dolls, one wearing Ákos's face; not yet confronted.

Places:
- Vaskapu: press-room, stone landing with lantern; Ákos's workbench and notebook (left there).
- Sallow: marsh town; Toll House at causeway's end; kitchen table where tagged things appear at dawn; iron Wheel under the floorboards; iron stove.
- The Point: burned roofless house, child-sized hearthstone; Ilka's smokehouse, shelf of faceless dolls; Sára and Ákos now at its door.

Things and rules:
1. The Table: dawn deliveries, paper tags in Sára's cramped hand, written at night and forgotten; ink can still be wet.
2. The Toll: taken from the deliverer — memory, skill, or sense; opening what you carry costs memory.
3. Dolls: faceless; a doll takes a face at the bell; the small doll is blank again, and now warms near the debt.
4. The Notebook: Ákos records every loss; fourteen dated names in Sára's hand; the sister's seventh, "I paid for him."
5. The Bell and the Wheel: bell rings at the hour of delivery, wherever the house chooses; refusal turns shavings cold and ashen, then warm once the debt is paid.
6. Refusal: does not stop the toll; moves payment to the named deliverer and adds a fee taken from the refuser.

Open threads:
- Why Sára makes the dolls and forgets; why she chose Ákos.
- The other thirteen names, and what each cost.
- Who died in the fire, and the child the hearthstone belonged to.
- The printer woman's lost name, and what she carried.
- Whether the sister's face can be won back.
- The doll at The Point wearing Ákos's face — what it means, and why Sára's blank doll now warms.
