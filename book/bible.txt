Characters:
- second writer — unnamed woman, bell in jaw, word SECOND worn into forearm, holds rule‑book, descended into chamber, now carries ledger; memory of Elias Vane.
- figure — pale, scarred, river‑coloured eyes, builder of room and clock, warns breaking rule costs remembering.
- fisherman — below the weir, turned, his face became a page folded into a boat, delivered new sentence “The second hand chooses the next turn.”
- first hand — Elias Vane, former scribe, now at threshold, seeks to preserve lost memory within looping structure.
- third hand — entity of wet pages and river ink, manifest through clock mouth, offers quill; now mostly still, offers wrist instead.

Places:
- Round room above the Weir: desk, Autos, slate, clock mouth sealed, no door.
- Harrow: near‑still river, quay, wet steps, fish stalls, silent bell tower.
- Wet steps: descend beneath the weir to the chamber.
- Deeper chamber: floor‑to‑ceiling desks with Autos, faint current beneath.
- Tunnel to chest: lantern‑lit passage ending at rope‑bound chest marked E.L.V.
- River stones downstream: site of missing bell’s mark.

Things and rules:
- Auto: blank until written; becomes real in Harrow; ink never dries; replicates across hands; ink can dry backwards to erase names.
- Rule‑book: last page flips at backward tick; lines become answers; records loop rules.
- Clock: fixed face, mouth opens/closes as door; ticks unevenly; admits river‑born ink; now sealed after loop.
- Bell in her jaw: rings when river shifts; tolled seven times; seventh toll summoned upward; now hums/vibrates when loop shifts.
- Word SECOND: worn on forearm, hot then cools; glows when loop active.
- Ledger “The First Hand’s Desire”: lists names, dates; glows for Elias Vane; shifts order when loop remembers.
- Floating page: bears first hand’s signature; rewrites itself; now reveals second writer as vessel of his memory.
- Auto as next hand: when an Auto is inscribed with a command, it becomes the active hand, reading the room and initiating the next turn.

Open threads:
- Why do countless Autos bear the same sentence?
- Who reads the room’s contents?
- What is the missing bell’s mark and its purpose?
- How does the loop’s resolution change the rule that must be broken?
